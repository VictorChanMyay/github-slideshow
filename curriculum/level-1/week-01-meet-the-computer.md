# Week 1: Meet the Computer

**Course:** 1.1 Computer Basics | **Level:** 1 | **Class length:** 90 minutes (+ 1 hour practice)

## Learning objectives
By the end of the week, learners can:
1. Name and explain the job of the main computer parts (input, output, processing, storage).
2. Tell hardware from software, with examples.
3. Compare desktops, laptops, tablets and phones.
4. Turn a computer on, log in, and shut it down safely.

## Materials
- One computer per 1–2 learners (a spare or opened-up old computer for show-and-tell, if available)
- Sticky notes or printed part labels
- Worksheet 1 (below), pens, whiteboard or flipchart

## Lesson plan

| Time | Activity | Details |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | **Warm-up: "What do you already know?"** | Ask: What do you use computers for? What parts can you name? List answers on the board. Note each learner's starting level. |
| 10–25 min | **Mini-lesson: How a computer works** | Use the "restaurant" analogy: **Input** = the customer's order (keyboard, mouse, microphone, touchscreen). **Processing** = the kitchen (CPU, memory/RAM). **Output** = the meal served (screen, speakers, printer). **Storage** = the pantry (hard drive/SSD, USB, cloud). |
| 25–40 min | **Hardware vs. software** | Hardware = you can touch it. Software = instructions that run on it (operating system, apps, games). Sort 12 cards (mouse, Word, screen, Chrome, keyboard, a game, speaker, Windows, USB, printer, Photoshop, battery) into two groups. |
| 40–60 min | **Activity: Label the computer** | In pairs, stick labels on a real computer (monitor, keyboard, mouse/touchpad, CPU/tower, speakers, power button, USB port, headphone jack, webcam). Then mark each as input, output, storage or processing. |
| 60–70 min | **Comparing devices** | Discuss desktop, laptop, tablet, smartphone: size, portability, power, typical use. Fill the comparison table on Worksheet 1. |
| 70–85 min | **Hands-on: power on, log in, shut down** | Demonstrate, then each pair practices: power on, log in, open and close a program, then Shut down (not just closing the lid or holding the power button). Explain why safe shutdown matters. |
| 85–90 min | **Exit ticket** | Each learner writes: 1 thing learned, 1 question. |

## Worksheet 1

**A. Match the part to its job**

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Keyboard | Shows pictures and text |
| Monitor | Lets you type letters and numbers |
| Mouse | Stores files permanently |
| Speakers | Moves the pointer and clicks |
| SSD / hard drive | Plays sound |
| CPU | The "brain" that follows instructions |

*(Answers: Keyboard→types; Monitor→shows; Mouse→pointer; Speakers→sound; Storage→files; CPU→brain.)*

## Practice at home or in the lab (about 1 hour)
1. List 10 devices at home or school that contain a computer (e.g., phone, TV, microwave, car, game console) and say what its input and output are.
2. Take a photo or draw a computer and label 6 parts.
3. Practice power on, log in and shut down 3 times without help.

## Assessment (checklist for Course 1.1 practical)
- [ ] Names at least 6 parts and their jobs
- [ ] Correctly sorts hardware and software
- [ ] Explains input, output, processing, storage
- [ ] Safely powers on, logs in and shuts down

## Teacher notes
- Mixed experience is normal: pair confident learners with beginners and rotate roles.
- Keep vocabulary simple (say "memory" and "brain" before "RAM" and "CPU"), then introduce the technical terms.
- Have a plan B if devices are limited: use printed pictures and take turns on the real machine.
- Common misconceptions: "the monitor is the computer", "closing the lid shuts it down", and "the cloud is not a real place". Address these as they come up.
