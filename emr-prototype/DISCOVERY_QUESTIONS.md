# Discovery Questions for Mae Tao Clinic (before building the EMR)

Purpose: learn how the clinic really works, what already exists, and what limits apply, so the EMR fits the clinic instead of the other way round. Answers update `PROPOSAL.md`, where items are still marked "to confirm".

How to use: start with the **First meeting (12 questions)**. Use the detailed sections afterwards with the right people. Mark each answer with who gave it and the date.

Also ask to see, not only be told: paper forms, registers, current HIS screens, donor report templates.

---

## First meeting (12 questions, with leadership)

1. What problem should the EMR solve first? (e.g., lost or duplicate records, slow reporting, continuity of care, donor reports)
2. Which departments should go first, and which later?
3. Who will own the EMR project and make decisions on the clinic's side?
4. What is the current Health Information System (HIS)? What software, who maintains it, and what data does it hold?
5. What did the Digitize project (paper-record digitization, Reproductive Health since 2019) deliver? Is it still used?
6. Which donors and partners need which reports, and how often? Can we see the templates?
7. How reliable are electricity and internet in each building?
8. What computers, tablets and phones do staff have today?
9. How are patients without ID registered today? Which ID types do patients carry?
10. What is the budget and timeline for licences (e.g., Retool), hosting and training? Who pays after the pilot?
11. Who decides data-sharing and privacy rules? Is there a data protection officer or a policy?
12. Can we have the official logo files, brand guidelines and the latest Annual Report?

---

## A. Scope and governance (leadership, Health Services Deputy Director)

- Which services exist today: outpatient, inpatient, reproductive health, child health, laboratory, blood bank, eye, dental, surgery, rehabilitation and prosthetics, child protection, outreach? (Sources conflict, so confirm each.)
- Which departments will not be in the EMR (e.g., child protection case files kept separately)?
- Who approves changes to forms, codes and reports after launch?
- What does success look like in 6 and 12 months?
- Is there a steering group? Who from clinical staff will be involved?
- What happens if the clinic loses funding or the developer leaves? Who keeps the code, data and documentation?
- Is there an existing IT or HIS policy, or an in-house Tech Team standard we must follow?

## B. Patients and registration (registration clerks, HIS officer)

- How are patients identified today (card, number, name)? How are duplicates found?
- Which names and scripts are recorded (Burmese, Karen, Thai, English)? Any spelling rules?
- How are ages recorded when the date of birth is unknown?
- What ID or card types exist (Thai ID, Myanmar ID, UNHCR/camp card, passport, none)?
- How are mothers and babies linked? Families or households?
- What registration data is sensitive (legal status, ethnicity, origin), and who may see it?
- Is a patient card, QR code or photo acceptable to patients and staff?
- Roughly how many new and returning patients register per day?

## C. Clinical workflow (doctors, nurses, medics; ask in each department)

- Walk through a typical patient visit from arrival to leaving. Where are the waiting points?
- What forms and registers are used in each department? Which fields matter, and which are never used?
- What diagnosis coding is used (ICD-10, local list, free text)? Who codes?
- What vital signs, growth measurements and screening are done at triage?
- Which clinical guidelines does the clinic follow (WHO, Thai MOPH, IMCI, others)? Should the EMR prompt for them?
- How are prescriptions written, and is there a clinic formulary (drug list)?
- How are referrals made to Mae Sot Hospital and others? What information goes with the patient, and how is feedback received?
- What happens at night and on weekends or in emergencies?
- What would a clinician hate to see in a new system?

## D. Reproductive, maternal and child health

- Which antenatal, delivery, postnatal and family planning forms or cards are used? Do patients keep their own card?
- Which danger signs and risk factors must raise an alert?
- How are births recorded and reported, and is there a birth notification process?
- Which immunization schedule is used, and who tracks overdue children?
- How are growth, nutrition and malnutrition programs recorded?
- What did the Digitize project change in this department, and what worked or failed?

## E. Infectious disease and special programs

- How are malaria, TB and HIV recorded and reported today?
- Which registers are required by partners or authorities?
- What confidentiality rules apply to HIV and other sensitive results?
- Is outbreak reporting needed (daily or weekly counts by diagnosis)?

## F. Laboratory, pharmacy, blood bank, supplies

- Which tests are done in-house? How are orders, specimens and results handled today?
- Are any lab instruments connected to computers?
- How is pharmacy stock counted, with batches and expiry? Who orders and who receives donations?
- Are drugs and supplies donor-restricted (must be reported by donor)?
- How is blood bank stock and donor/recipient information recorded?

## G. Reporting and donors

- List every regular report: audience, frequency, format, deadline, who prepares it, and how long it takes.
- Which indicators need patient-level data, and which only counts?
- Are reports submitted to Thai health authorities? Which, and in what format?
- Is DHIS2 or any other platform used or required by partners?

## H. Technology, infrastructure and support

- Where would data be hosted (local server, cloud, which country)? Any restrictions from donors or law?
- What are the current backup practices? When was a restore last tested?
- What internet speed is available, and how often does it fail? Are there local-network-only options?
- What licences exist (e.g., Microsoft)? Is Retool approved, and for how many users?
- What can the in-house Tech Team support, and who answers calls when the system is down?
- What is the paper fallback during outages?

## I. People, language and training

- Roughly how many staff will use the system, by role and department?
- What is their comfort with computers? Who could be a department "super-user"?
- Which language(s) should the screens, forms and printouts use?
- How much training time can staff spare? What format works (short sessions, floor-walking, handouts)?
- Are there literacy or accessibility needs to consider?

## J. Privacy, legal and ethics

- Who is the data controller? Is there a consent process for treatment and for data use?
- What are the rules for sharing data with donors, partners and Thai authorities?
- What risks do patients face if data is exposed (legal status, protection cases)? What must never be recorded or shared?
- Is there an incident response plan, including notifying the regulator within 72 hours if a breach affects personal data?
- Who approves user accounts, and how are leavers removed?
- Has the clinic done any data protection or security review?

## K. Data migration and history

- How many paper records exist, where are they stored, and for how many years?
- Which old records must be digitized, and which can stay on paper?
- What is in the existing HIS that must be imported? In what format?
- How long must records be kept?

---

## Things to bring or request

- Samples of each paper form and register (blank copies; no patient data)
- Screenshots or export from the current HIS (no patient data)
- Last Annual Report and one donor report template
- Drug formulary and laboratory test list
- Staff list by role (counts only) and building map
- Official logo files and brand guidelines
- Any existing IT, privacy or HIS policy

## Ground rules for the conversations

- Use blank or fake data. Do not accept or copy real patient records during discovery.
- Write down who said what and when. Confirm numbers against a document before using them in a proposal.
- Share notes back with the clinic so they can correct mistakes.
