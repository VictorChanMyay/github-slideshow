# Draft email to the clinic (edit before sending)

Fill the brackets. Send it yourself from your own address; keep it short. Attach nothing with patient data (there is none). Consider attaching `docs/HANDOVER.md` as a PDF, or just offer it.

**To:** [name and address of the person you were advised to contact: leadership, Health Services lead, or Tech Team]
**Subject:** Volunteer offer: a free electronic medical record prototype for Mae Tao Clinic

Dear [Name],

My name is [your name]. I teach IT at [school name] in Mae Sot, and I work legally in Thailand under my teaching visa. I admire the clinic's work for people from Myanmar, and I would like to contribute my skills as a volunteer, without payment.

To be clear about what this is: I am **not** applying for a job. I read about the clinic's search for a software developer and wanted to offer something different, in my own time.

Over the past weeks I built a **working prototype of an electronic medical record system**, using **only invented demo data**. It covers registration, outpatient visits, laboratory, pharmacy and stock, antenatal and child health, TB/malaria/other programmes, referrals, wards, a blood bank, and donor-style reports. It works without internet, in English now, with Burmese and Karen text planned once native speakers can review it. It uses no real patient information, and I will not use any unless the clinic approves in writing.

I would be grateful for **30 minutes** to show it to you or your team, and to listen. I would like to learn how the clinic really works, what is useful, and what is not. If it is not useful, that is a perfectly good answer. I would also welcome the clinic's Tech Team reviewing the work, since I believe the clinic should own and understand anything built for it.

I can come to the clinic or meet online, at a time that suits you. I can also send a short summary, and a copy of the working demo, before we meet.

Thank you very much for considering this.

Kind regards,
[Name]
[Position, school]
[Phone / email]

## Notes before you send
- Check the facts in the email against what you can confirm: the job posting, who to address, your visa status wording (only what is true for you).
- Do not promise dates, features or support after you leave; the meeting is for listening.
- Ask whether the clinic wants a short **volunteer agreement** (confidentiality, ownership of the code, no real data until approved).
- A follow-up after about one week is polite; after two reminders, let it go.
